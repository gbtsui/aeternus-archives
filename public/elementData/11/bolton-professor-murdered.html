Renowned Linguistics Professor Killed on Bolton Campus Professor Solomon Haruspex, a senior linguistics researcher at the University of Bolton, was killed on his campus earlier this week. On October 16th, 1367, Dr. Haruspex and his daughter Sofia were seen leaving his office in the O'Brien Hall with 2 unidentified men, talking amicably. Later, a commotion occurred in the alley between the Hall and the Engineering Technology Building, alerting campus police to the area. Witnesses reported hearing multiple gunshots before officers arrived. Dr. Haruspex was found on the ground in a pool of blood surrounded by his companions. He was rushed to St. George's Hospital, where he was pronounced dead. The cause of death was a stab wound to the abdomen. Police confirmed that Haruspex’s daughter was not injured. Authorities declined to comment on the identities of the two men, citing an ongoing ecclesial inquiry. No individual was seen entering or exiting the alleyway on available surveillance footage. Haruspex served as head of the Center for Historical Linguistics and Information Discovery at Bolton (CHILD@B). With no remaining active members, the university has begun dissolving the research group and reallocating its resources. CHILD@B had recently published several controversial papers examining state narratives in post-war archival records. Over the past 3 months, the center had reduced staff and cancelled multiple research grants. “We are cooperating fully with authorities,” a university spokesperson said. "Dr. Haruspex was a cherished member of our faculty and we will investigate the circumstances of his death to the fullest."
